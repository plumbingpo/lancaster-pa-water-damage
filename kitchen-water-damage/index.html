Kitchen Water Damage Help in Lancaster

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. Our Lancaster crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Warning Signs

  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Vinyl floor that has discolored or gone soft by the cabinets or the refrigerator
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Refrigerator Water Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Toe Kick Space

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
